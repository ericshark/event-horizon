    THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE JOURNEY

    Sources & methodology

    This is a guided illustration, not a mission trajectory. Object sizes, spacing, rotation and transit time are rescaled for readability. Motion defaults to the system preference and can be set to System, Full, or Reduced at any time.

    Reference snapshot · 29 October 2026

    Planet centres are reproducible, low-precision positions from published J2000 Kepler elements propagated to this date, in a Sun-centred ecliptic frame, then translated relative to Earth. Direct JPL Horizons retrieval was unavailable during preparation; these are explicitly not Horizons vectors or navigation-grade positions. The Moon uses mean distance. Voyager’s distance is a NASA-baseline estimate, with an illustrative direction. Stellar distances and directions are approximate catalogue references, held fixed during this virtual journey.

    Distance, time, and what 1c means

    Direct Earth distance is straight-line displacement. Tour distance is sampled along a curved route through the stops; it is longer because of detours. Light-equivalent time is tour distance ÷ 299,792.458 km/s. A massive spacecraft cannot reach light speed. Parker times assume its record speed is sustained indefinitely, without acceleration, braking or fuel limits. Displayed values are rounded; they are not mission forecasts or a traveller’s proper time.

    Sagittarius A* and the horizon model

    We use approximately 4 million solar masses at 26,000 light-years, with J2000 RA 17h45m40s, Dec −29°00′28″. A nonrotating Schwarzschild model gives a horizon radius of about 11.8 million km. The scientific observation radius is 1,000 horizon radii; optical framing is rescaled independently. Spin is omitted. The bright ring is an illustration, not a visible-light photograph. The twenty-second horizon descent and dark interior are illustrative.
