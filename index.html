Event Horizon

Chapter I Approach

Object HZ‑1 holds four million suns inside a sphere smaller than the orbit of Mercury. You are thirty Schwarzschild radii out, and already falling.

That ring behind it is the title of this page. Its light went around the back of the hole to reach you.

The disk

Gas spirals inward at a large fraction of light speed, heated past ten million kelvin. The side racing toward you burns blue-white; the side falling away reddens and dims.

Chapter III Photon sphere

Light, orbiting

At one and a half Schwarzschild radii, a photon can circle the hole and come back to where it started. Every thin ring you see is the disk again, lensed over the top, under the bottom, around the back.

Chapter IV Tides

Your feet are closer than your head, so they fall faster. Near a small black hole that difference pulls you into a thread long before the horizon. Around a giant like this one you cross the edge intact. The stretching comes later.

Chapter V Time

Two clocks

Each of your seconds lasts 1.02 seconds at home.

Watched from far away, you slow, redden, and freeze at the edge. You never see yourself cross. They never see you arrive.

Chapter VI Singularity

No light, no here
