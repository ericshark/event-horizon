## Verification record

- `npx tsc --noEmit` / `npm run build` — passed (existing >500 kB chunk warning remains).
- `PW_HEADLESS=true npx playwright test` (desktop 1440×1000 + mobile 390×844, headless Chromium, SwiftShader): the new `tests/planets.spec.ts` (provenance, preload lifecycle, Saturn clearance, ten worlds × rest/maximum zoom × two viewports, real-time Jupiter→Saturn flight) all pass. Full-suite run: 70 of 72 passed; the two failures were the long controlled-clock traversal tests exceeding their 90 s default because software WebGL is slower with 4K textures. Their timeouts were raised to 240 s and both passed on re-run (desktop project: 8/8).
- Captures: `artifacts/eh-03/` — all worlds at rest and maximum zoom (desktop/mobile) and three real-time frames of Jupiter→Saturn. Reviewed Earth, Moon, Venus, Sun, Saturn at rest and maximum zoom on desktop, and Saturn on mobile.
- Verification servers/browsers stopped; no processes left running.

## Limitations

- Chromium uses software WebGL here, so frame rates and pacing on real GPUs are not measured; the 4K textures made software rendering visibly slower. Mobile memory/bandwidth use of ≈20 MB of new imagery is unbenchmarked.
- Uranus and Neptune use 2048² maps (the source's highest resolution), so close inspection is softer than for the 4096² worlds. Mercury, Moon and Mars relief is a bump derived from albedo, not true elevation data. Ring brightness and Earth's cloud shadows are artistic, not radiometric.
- The Earth halo, Sun corona and ring edges are lightly aliased in software rendering. Motion was sampled, not reviewed frame-by-frame for hitches.
- No deployment or push.
